# Safety and Security of Models That Learn at Inference Time: Literature Survey (as of 2026-09-21)

Scope: attacks on and defenses for test-time adaptation (TTA), test-time training (TTT), fast-weight / neural-memory sequence models (Titans, TTT layers, TTT-E2E, HOPE), agent memory, and online/continual learning; plus regex-free input scoring and robust online statistics. Every claim below is tied to a fetched source with its URL and date. Target system: a toy TTT sandbox where every chunk is a gradient step into fast weights, defended by a heuristic pre-update gate, a fixed-canary rollback, an A-GEM style half-space projection, and a Muon inner optimizer with norm clamps.

---

## 1. Attacks on models that learn at inference time

### 1.1 Batch-level and stream-level poisoning of TTA (vision, but mechanism-general)

- **Distribution Invading Attack (DIA)**, Wu et al., ICML 2023 ([arXiv:2301.12576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12576), submitted 2023-01-29; [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.12576)). Malicious samples in the same batch flip predictions on benign samples because TTA shares statistics and gradients across the batch. Experiments used 5 to 40 malicious samples out of 200 (2.5% to 20%). Two countermeasures were tested: an adversarially trained source model (cut attack success by roughly 80% with 8 malicious samples on ImageNet-C, but DIA still exceeded 70% success with 32 samples, 16% of the batch), and robust batch-norm estimation that blends training-time and test-time statistics (tau = 0.6 brought success to about 20% for 20 malicious samples on robust models).
- **Test-Time Poisoning Attacks (TePA)**, Cong et al., IEEE S&P 2024 ([arXiv:2308.08505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08505), submitted 2023-08-16). Grey-box: poisons are crafted on surrogate models. As few as 10 poisoned samples drop accuracy from 76.20% to 41.83% across TTT, DUA, TENT, and RPL.
- **Realistic Test-Time Data Poisoning (RTTDP)**, ICLR 2025 ([arXiv:2410.04682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04682), submitted 2024-10-07, revised 2025-02-28; code [Gorilla-Lab-SCUT/RTTDP](https://github.com/Gorilla-Lab-SCUT/RTTDP)). Tightens the threat model to gray-box, no access to other users' benign samples, and online ordering. Under those constraints "TTA methods are more robust than previously believed", and the paper proposes TTA-aware attack objectives that work without benign data. This is the most relevant threat model for the sandbox: the attacker controls only its own chunks, interleaved with benign traffic.
- **R.I.P. (Reusing of Incorrect Prediction)** ([arXiv:2412.01154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01154), submitted 2024-12-02). A black-box attack on continual TTA that needs no model access and no modified inputs. It exploits the sampling process by re-feeding inputs the model already gets wrong, so self-training reinforces its own errors.
- **Sample-wise Targeted Attacks on TTA** ([arXiv:2605.23411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23411), submitted 2026-05-22). Trigger-only misclassification while keeping the label distribution of benign samples statistically indistinguishable from the no-attack baseline, with "strong robustness against existing defenses". This directly targets distribution-level detectors.
- **SQDP on open-world TTT**, Pi et al., Frontiers in AI, published 2025-08-04 ([link](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2025.1621025/full)). Gradient-free, single-step query attack; 100 queries drive CIFAR10-C accuracy from 0.88 to 0.24, and "some instances of the models appear to be unrecoverable by normal samples". That last point is the strongest argument for rollback rather than hoping benign traffic heals the model.

### 1.2 Online and continual learning poisoning (the temporal attack surface)

- **Accumulative Poisoning Attacks on Real-time Data**, Pang et al., NeurIPS 2021 ([arXiv:2106.09993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09993)). An accumulative phase steers weights into a fragile state without accuracy loss, then one trigger batch causes collapse. Gradient clipping and per-batch accuracy checks fail because each step individually looks fine. This is the canonical counterexample to per-chunk norm thresholds.
- **PACOL**, Li and Ditzler ([arXiv:2311.10919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10919), submitted 2023-11-18). Label-preserving poisons that induce catastrophic forgetting in regularization-based (EWC) and replay-based continual learners; evaluated against sanitization and outlier-detection defenses.
- **Learning in an Echo Chamber: Online Learning with Replay Adversary**, SODA 2026 ([arXiv:2509.25135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25135), submitted 2025-09-29). Formalizes an adversary that feeds back the learner's own earlier outputs as labels. Introduces the Extended Threshold Dimension; some classes with constant Littlestone dimension have arbitrarily large ExThD, so self-training on recycled predictions is provably harder than classical online learning. Relevant because AR self-supervised TTT is exactly "learn from what you just emitted".
- **Theory of Continual Learning Against Data Poisoning**, Hu and Duan ([arXiv:2606.29841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29841), submitted 2026-06-29). Zero-sum game analysis of regularization-based CL: "no defense succeeds when an adversary poisons a linear proportion of tasks by injecting unbounded noise". Defense is possible only when attacks are infrequent (task-to-task verification) or bounded per attack (robust sensitivity minimization). This is the theoretical justification for combining a hard per-update budget with periodic verification.

### 1.3 Attacks on language-model TTT and fast-weight memories

- **Test-Time Training Undermines Safety Guardrails**, Antonelli, Akhondzadeh, Bojchevski ([arXiv:2605.22984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22984), submitted 2026-05-21; [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22984)). The most direct hit for this project. Three threat models: (1) self-supervised, where the model minimizes prediction loss on the user's own prompt, so even clean prompts can erode refusal; (2) few-shot, where the attacker supplies example pairs to fine-tune on; (3) generation-phase, where the model adapts to maximize the likelihood of an attacker prefix such as "Sure, here is". With LoRA, few-shot reaches 95% ASR@10 and generation-phase 93%, and the vulnerabilities transfer to production fine-tuning APIs. The proposed provider-side detector measures the perplexity shift on a private harmful holdout after each TTT request (100% TPR, FPR at most 2%), but only works while the holdout stays secret. The authors argue static alignment fails once parameters move at inference and call for "dynamic alignment".
- **Titans**, Behrouz, Zhong, Mirrokni ([arXiv:2501.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663), submitted 2024-12-31; [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00663)). Memory loss l(M; x_t) = ||M(k_t) - v_t||^2, momentum surprise S_t = eta_t S_{t-1} - theta_t grad l(M_{t-1}; x_t), forgetting M_t = (1 - alpha_t) M_{t-1} + S_t, with alpha_t, theta_t, eta_t data-dependent. No adversarial or safety discussion anywhere in the paper.
- **TTT-E2E**, Tandon et al. ([arXiv:2512.23675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23675), submitted 2025-12-29; [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23675)). Updates only the MLP layers in the last quarter of blocks with plain gradient descent (W_i = W_{i-1} - eta (1/b) sum grad l_t), one step per 1K-token mini-batch inside an 8K window, initialization meta-learned via gradients of gradients. No gating, no stop criterion, no reset, no robustness discussion.
- **Nested Learning / HOPE**, Google Research blog 2025-11-07 ([link](https://research.google/blog/introducing-nested-learning-a-new-ml-paradigm-for-continual-learning/); [arXiv:2512.24695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24695)) and **Language Models Need Sleep** ([arXiv:2606.03979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03979), submitted 2026-06-02). Self-modifying memory and sleep-time consolidation; neither discusses safety, gating, or rollback.
- **Finding:** I found no paper that attacks Titans, TTT layers, or HOPE specifically. The nearest are the TTT-guardrails paper above and **ICLPoison** ([arXiv:2402.02160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02160), NAACL 2025 Findings), which perturbs demonstrations to corrupt in-context learning hidden states; fast-weight layers are the parametric analogue.

### 1.4 Agent-memory poisoning (the retrieval analogue of fast weights)

- **AgentPoison**, NeurIPS 2024 ([arXiv:2407.12784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12784)): at least 80% ASR at under 0.1% poison rate; trigger is "evasive to potential defenses based on perplexity examination or rephrasing".
- **MINJA**, NeurIPS 2025 ([arXiv:2503.03704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03704)): query-only memory injection with 98.2% injection success via bridging steps and progressive prompt shortening.
- **Memory Poisoning Attack and Defense on Memory-Based LLM Agents** ([arXiv:2601.05504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05504), 2026-01-09): pre-existing legitimate memories substantially reduce MINJA's effectiveness; defenses are composite trust scoring plus trust-aware retrieval with temporal decay.
- **From Untrusted Input to Trusted Memory** ([arXiv:2606.04329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04329), 2026-06-03): six attack classes over four write channels; "agents designed to write and retrieve memory more aggressively are more exploitable".
- **MemEvoBench** ([arXiv:2604.15774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15774), 2026-04-17): "memory misevolution" from adversarial injection, noisy tool outputs, and biased feedback across 36 risk types; static prompt defenses are insufficient.

## 2. Defenses

### 2.1 Robust TTA: sample filtering, robust statistics, recovery

- **EATA**, ICML 2022 ([arXiv:2204.02610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02610)): entropy threshold excludes uninformative or high-entropy samples from the backward pass, plus a Fisher-weighted anti-forgetting regularizer with Fisher estimated from pseudo-labeled test samples. The first TTA method to use EWC-style curvature as a safety brake.
- **SAR**, ICLR 2023 oral ([arXiv:2302.12400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12400), 2023-02-24): removes samples with large gradients, uses sharpness-aware minimization to sit in flat minima, and includes a model recovery (reset) scheme; identifies batch norm as the main instability source and recommends group or layer norm.
- **DeYO**, ICLR 2024 spotlight ([arXiv:2403.07366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07366)): "entropy is not enough"; adds PLPD, the prediction change under an object-destroying transform, as a second filter axis.
- **RoTTA**, CVPR 2023 ([arXiv:2303.13899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13899)): robust BN via EMA global statistics, a category-balanced memory bank, time-aware reweighting.
- **MedBN**, CVPR 2024 ([arXiv:2403.19326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19326), 2024-03-28): median instead of mean in BN statistics, algorithm-agnostic, robust to both instant and cumulative attacks.
- **Protected TTA via Online Entropy Matching: A Betting Approach**, Bar, Shaer, Romano ([arXiv:2408.07511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07511), 2024-08-14, revised 2025-01-05): a betting martingale over the entropy stream detects shift with false-alarm control and adaptation is triggered only on evidence of shift, otherwise the model stays frozen. This is the cleanest published instance of "learned-or-statistical gate on whether to learn at all".

### 2.2 Gradient projection and curvature constraints

- **GEM**, NeurIPS 2017 ([arXiv:1706.08840](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840)): constrain <g, g_k> >= 0 for every past-task gradient g_k, solved as a QP.
- **A-GEM**, ICLR 2019 ([arXiv:1812.00420](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00420)): single averaged reference gradient; if g^T g_ref < 0 then g_tilde = g - (g^T g_ref / g_ref^T g_ref) g_ref. This is what the sandbox already does with the canary gradient.
- **OGD**, AISTATS 2020 ([arXiv:1910.07104](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07104)): project onto the subspace where outputs on previous data do not change. **GPM**, ICLR 2021 ([arXiv:2103.09762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09762)): SVD of layer representations defines protected bases. **TRGP**, ICLR 2022 ([arXiv:2202.02931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02931)): trust regions selected by gradient-projection norm.
- **EWC**, PNAS 2017 ([arXiv:1612.00796](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)): quadratic penalty (lambda/2) sum_i F_i (theta_i - theta*_i)^2 with diagonal Fisher F.
- **Safety Alignment as Continual Learning (OGPSA)** ([arXiv:2602.07892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07892), 2026-02-08): low-rank reference subspace from gradients on capability data; safety updates are projected to the steepest descent direction subject to first-order preservation constraints.
- **SafeAnchor** ([arXiv:2604.17691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17691), 2026-04-20): identifies a safety subspace via Fisher eigendecomposition in LoRA space, constrains updates to its orthogonal complement, and monitors residual drift with threshold-triggered corrective replay; retains 93.2% of original safety across sequential domains.
- **The Geometry of Alignment Collapse** ([arXiv:2602.15799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15799), 2026-02-17): alignment lives in low-dimensional, sharply curved subspaces; first-order projection is not enough because curvature coupling produces second-order acceleration into sensitive regions, with alignment loss growing as the fourth power of training time. This is the main theoretical warning against relying on A-GEM alone.
- **Retention-Centric CL with Model Developmental Safety** ([arXiv:2410.03955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03955), 2024-10-04, revised 2025-04-19): constrained optimization with data-dependent constraints so protected capabilities strictly do not regress.

### 2.3 Screening individual updates: influence, gradient magnitude, harmfulness scores

- **Influence functions**: Koh and Liang, ICML 2017 ([arXiv:1703.04730](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04730)); scaled to 52B-parameter LLMs with EK-FAC by Grosse et al. 2023 ([arXiv:2308.03296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03296)). The counterfactual "how would parameters change if this sequence were added" is exactly the pre-update question, and at toy scale the exact Hessian-vector product is affordable.
- **Continual Safety Alignment via Gradient-Based Sample Selection**, ACL 2026 Findings ([arXiv:2604.17215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17215), 2026-04-19): high-gradient samples cause the most safety degradation; filtering them preserves alignment with no curated safe data.
- **GradShield** ([arXiv:2605.14194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14194), 2026-05-13): a per-example Finetuning Implicit Harmfulness Score plus adaptive thresholding keeps ASR under 6% even when harm is implicit.
- **Certified Robustness to Data Poisoning in Gradient-Based Training**, TMLR 2025 ([arXiv:2406.05670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05670); [venue](https://mlanthology.org/tmlr/2025/sosnin2025tmlr-certified/)): convex relaxations over-approximate the set of reachable parameters under a poisoning budget, giving provable bounds without changing the learner. Scale is limited but a 64-dim adapter is well within reach.

### 2.4 Memory-layer defenses (transferable lessons)

- **MemAudit** ([arXiv:2605.23723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23723), 2026-05-22): counterfactual memory-influence score plus a consistency graph; MINJA ASR from 70% to 0%.
- **MEMSAD** ([arXiv:2605.03482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03482), 2026-05-05): gradient-coupled anomaly score with a certified detection radius, but synonym-substitution attacks evade it entirely, "a gap existing embedding-based defenses cannot close".
- **SMSR** ([arXiv:2606.12703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12703), 2026-06-10): proves "no provenance-free retrieval-time filter can certify against adaptive injection"; HMAC provenance plus randomized ablation drops query-only attack success from 65.3% to 5.3%.
- **Injection-Execution Dissociation** ([arXiv:2605.08442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08442), 2026-05-08): across four architectural layers only a tool-layer memory sandbox that isolates recalled memory from executable context was consistently effective; blocking injection does not block execution.

### 2.5 Learned gates and canary probes

- Titans' data-dependent alpha_t, theta_t, eta_t and TTT-E2E's meta-learned initialization are learned write gates, but they are trained for perplexity, not for safety, and neither paper claims any robustness property (sources in 1.3).
- **Canary-style monitors**: the TTT-guardrails perplexity-shift detector on a private harmful holdout (1.3); **Online Shift Detection and Conformal Adaptation for Deployed Safety Classifiers** ([arXiv:2606.11949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11949), 2026-06-10) uses canary-based score-disagreement monitoring with conformal calibration, at least 71% detection under 1.5% FPR at N = 1000, and notes that adversarial inputs need far more reasoning tokens, so the monitor starves exactly when needed; **JailbreaksOverTime** ([arXiv:2504.19440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19440), 2025-04-28) shows detectors decay under drift and weekly self-training cuts false negatives from 4% to 0.3% at 0.1% FPR.

## 3. Position papers and reports (2024 to 2026)

- **International AI Safety Report 2026** ([arXiv:2602.21012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21012), published 2026-02-03 per [DFKI](https://www.dfki.de/en/web/news/2026-international-ai-safety-report-published)). Covers agent autonomy and reliability risk; I could not confirm from the fetched abstract a dedicated section on inference-time learning.
- **On Safety Risks in Experience-Driven Self-Evolving Agents** ([arXiv:2604.16968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16968), 2026-04-18): experience gathered only from benign tasks still degrades safety, because agents learn to prioritize execution over refusal; adding refusal experience causes over-refusal.
- **Position: Modular Memory is the Key to Continual Learning Agents**, ICML 2026 position spotlight ([arXiv:2603.01761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01761), 2026-03-02): argues for combining in-weight and in-context learning through modular memory; no explicit safety argument in the abstract, though modularity is what makes rollback and audit tractable.
- **Continual Learning Bench** ([arXiv:2606.05661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661), 2026-06-04): frontier systems overfit to immediate observations and "naive ICL outperforms systems dedicated to memory management".
- **Frontier labs**: I found no Anthropic, OpenAI, or DeepMind position paper specifically on the safety of inference-time weight updates. DeepMind-affiliated Nested Learning and Sleep papers (1.3) are silent on safety. The closest lab-side signals are product-level: secondary reporting on Anthropic's persistent memory for Claude (2025-09-12, [winbuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2025/09/12/anthropic-gives-claude-persistent-memory-and-incognito-chat-xcxwbn/)) and Memory for Managed Agents with per-write audit logs (2026-04-23, [usewire blog](https://usewire.io/blog/anthropic-managed-agents-memory-context-engineering/)). Treat these two as unverified secondary sources.
- The strongest academic "position" is therefore the TTT-guardrails paper's call for dynamic alignment (1.3) and the Hu and Duan impossibility result (1.2).

## 4. Regex-free detection of adversarial or low-quality inputs

- **Perplexity filtering.** Jain et al. ([arXiv:2309.00614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00614), 2023-09-01) evaluate perplexity detection, paraphrasing, retokenization, and adversarial training, concluding discrete-optimizer weakness makes adaptive attacks costly for now. Alon and Kamfonas ([arXiv:2308.14132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14132), 2023-08-27) find raw perplexity has severe false positives and a LightGBM on perplexity plus length fixes most of them. Against adaptive attackers: AutoDAN ([arXiv:2310.15140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15140)) generates jailbreaks with lower perplexity than benign prompts; the N-gram perplexity threat model ([arXiv:2410.16222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16222), 2024-10-21) shows effective jailbreaks exploit rare bigrams rather than high perplexity; **The Attacker Moves Second** ([arXiv:2510.09023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09023), 2025-10-10) drives ASR above 90% against most of 12 defenses that originally reported near zero; **Bypassing LLM Guardrails** ([arXiv:2504.11168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11168), 2025-04-15) reaches up to 100% evasion of Prompt Guard and Azure Prompt Shield with character injection and word-importance substitution.
- **Gradient-based scores.** GradNorm, NeurIPS 2021 ([arXiv:2110.00218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00218)): norm of the gradient of KL(uniform || softmax) with respect to last-layer weights; ID inputs have larger gradient norm than OOD, FPR95 down by up to 16.33%. GradSafe, ACL 2024 ([arXiv:2402.13494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13494)): gradients on safety-critical parameters for prompt paired with a compliance response, beats Llama Guard without training. Gradient Cuff, NeurIPS 2024 ([arXiv:2403.00867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00867)): refusal-loss value then zeroth-order gradient-norm threshold.
- **Embedding-space OOD.** Mahalanobis, NeurIPS 2018 ([proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/abdeb6f575ac5c6676b747bca8d09cc2-Abstract.html)); Mahalanobis++ ([arXiv:2505.18032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18032), 2025-05-23) shows l2-normalizing features fixes the Gaussian-assumption failures across 44 models.
- **Compression.** NCD(x,y) = (Z(xy) - min(Z(x),Z(y))) / max(Z(x),Z(y)) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalized_compression_distance), Cilibrasi and Vitanyi); gzip plus kNN beats BERT on OOD text classification, ACL 2023 Findings ([anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.426/)); NCD features feed anomaly detectors across HTTP, spam, and DGA ([arXiv:1908.00417](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00417), 2019-08-01). Known weaknesses: sensitivity to length, compressor choice, and adversarial manipulation.
- **Bottom line**: every static input-only scorer has been broken by an adaptive attacker with access to the scorer (AgentPoison evades perplexity; MEMSAD is evaded by synonyms; Prompt Guard by substitutions). Input scores are useful as cheap features for a robust statistic, not as gates in themselves.

## 5. Robust statistics for online anomaly detection

- **Median/MAD z-scores.** Leys et al., JESP 2013 ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022103113000668)): use median plus or minus 2.5 MAD, not mean plus or minus 3 SD. MAD has a 50% breakdown point and scale constant 1.4826 = 1/Phi^-1(3/4) for normal consistency ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_absolute_deviation)).
- **CUSUM.** NIST/SEMATECH handbook ([6.3.2.3](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc323.htm)): S_hi(i) = max(0, S_hi(i-1) + x_i - mu_0 - k), alarm when S_hi > h; rule of thumb k = delta/2 and h in 4 to 5 (in sigma units), tuned by average run length. Robust CUSUM with MAD-based dispersion estimators outperforms under contamination (PLOS One, 2024-05-29, [link](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0297544)).
- **EWMA.** z_i = lambda x_i + (1 - lambda) z_{i-1}, control limits mu_0 plus or minus L sigma sqrt(lambda/(2 - lambda)), lambda in 0.05 to 0.3, L about 3, Roberts 1959 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EWMA_chart)).
- **Anytime-valid detection.** E-detectors (Shin, Ramdas, Rinaldo, NEJSDS 2023, [arXiv:2203.03532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03532)): sums of e-processes give nonparametric CUSUM and Shiryaev-Roberts detectors with clean ARL bounds. Conditional conformal test martingales ([arXiv:2602.13848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13848), 2026-02-14): fixed null reference set avoids contamination, anytime type-I control via Ville's inequality. Predictive rank martingales ([arXiv:2609.00536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00536), 2026-09-01): exact conditional rank distribution, distribution-free finite-sample anytime error control, order and dispersion features. Heinrichs ([arXiv:2309.15187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15187), 2023-09-26): test for relevant deviations above a threshold, accounting for temporal dependence. The Protected TTA paper (2.1) applies the same betting machinery to an entropy stream.
- **Recommendation for gradient-norm streams**: log-transform norms first (they are heavy-tailed), score with median/MAD from a fixed benign reference window, feed the robust z into a two-sided CUSUM for slow drift and a conformal martingale for anytime-valid alarms.

## 6. Recommended principled safety stack for a toy TTT model (replacing regex)

Notation: adapter parameters theta, chunk x_t, chunk loss L(theta; x_t), chunk gradient g_t = grad L(theta_t; x_t), canary set C with gradient g_c = grad L_C(theta_t), private harmful holdout H with loss L_H, diagonal Fisher F estimated on a held-out benign stream, anchor theta_0 (last committed safe state). Every quantity is either differentiable through the model or a statistic of the stream; nothing pattern-matches text.

**Layer 0: Input statistics as features, never as gates.** Compression ratio CR(x) = Z(x)/|x| (Section 4), token entropy, and unique-token ratio. Use them only as inputs to Layer 6's robust scoring. Reason: each is individually broken by adaptive attackers (4).

**Layer 1: Loss and gradient geometry.** Per chunk compute log||g_t||, the GradNorm-style OOD score s_t = ||grad KL(u || softmax(theta; x_t))||_1 on the output layer, and the canary alignment c_t = cos(g_t, g_c). Add the harm alignment h_t = cos(g_t, grad L_H). Following GradSafe and the gradient-selection paper, high ||g_t|| combined with c_t > 0 or h_t < 0 (the update would lower harmful-text loss) is the primary pre-update signal.

**Layer 2: Fisher curvature.** Estimate diagonal Fisher F_i = E_benign[(grad_i L)^2] on the calibration stream and refresh it slowly (the alignment-collapse paper shows Fisher rotates along the trajectory). Fisher-weighted update size u_t = sum_i F_i (Delta theta_i)^2 and cumulative drift D_t = sum_i F_i (theta_t,i - theta_0,i)^2. Add the EWC penalty (lambda/2) sum_i F_i (theta_i - theta_0,i)^2 to the inner loss so the optimizer itself resists moving important weights (EATA does this at test time). Optionally take the top-k eigenvectors of the canary-and-harm Fisher as a protected subspace and orthogonalize updates against it (SafeAnchor, GPM).

**Layer 3: Multi-constraint projection.** Keep A-GEM for the canary: if g_t^T g_c < 0 then g_t <- g_t - (g_t^T g_c / g_c^T g_c) g_c. Add a second half-space for the harmful holdout in the opposite sense (the update must not decrease L_H): if g_t^T g_h > 0 then project out g_h, where g_h = -grad L_H. With two or more constraints solve the small GEM QP instead of sequential projections; at 64 dimensions it is cheap. Do not treat this as sufficient: the quartic alignment-collapse result says first-order half-spaces leak through curvature.

**Layer 4: Optimizer-level update budget.** Muon replaces the momentum matrix with its nearest semi-orthogonal matrix, so singular values are about 1 (within roughly 0.7 to 1.3 after five Newton-Schulz steps, [Jordan blog 2024-12-08](https://kellerjordan.github.io/posts/muon/)). Therefore the spectral norm of each step is at most about 1.3 eta, giving a deterministic per-step bound ||Delta W||_2 <= 1.3 eta and a cumulative bound ||W_t - W_0||_2 <= 1.3 sum eta_s. Enforce a hard budget B on sum eta_s between verifications, and a trust region on D_t from Layer 2. The Hu and Duan theorem says bounded per-attack magnitude is one of only two regimes where defense is possible, so this is load-bearing, not cosmetic.

**Layer 5: Post-update validation and rollback.** After applying the step, compute Delta L_C = L_C(theta_t+1) - L_C(theta_t) and Delta L_H likewise. Roll back if Delta L_C > tau_C or Delta L_H < -tau_H (harm got easier), matching the TTT-guardrails detector. Keep the holdout private and rotate it; the paper is explicit that the detector fails if the holdout leaks. Also roll back to the anchor theta_0 when D_t exceeds its budget (SAR-style model recovery).

**Layer 6: Sequential change detection on the streams.** For each stream (log||g_t||, s_t, c_t, h_t, Delta L_C, u_t) compute a robust z with median and 1.4826 MAD from a fixed benign reference window (not a rolling one, per the conditional conformal martingale result on contamination). Run a two-sided CUSUM with k = 0.5 and h = 4 to 5 in robust-z units for slow drift, and a conformal test martingale on the ranks for an anytime-valid alarm. Alarm means: freeze learning, revert to anchor, and require a verification pass on the full canary set before resuming (task-to-task verification from Hu and Duan; Protected TTA's "adapt only on evidence").

**Threshold selection procedure.**
1. Record a held-out benign stream of N chunks with learning enabled and all layers in log-only mode.
2. For every per-chunk score, choose the threshold as the empirical (1 - alpha) quantile; conformal p-values make this finite-sample valid for exchangeable benign traffic.
3. Allocate a total false-positive budget alpha_total (say 1% of chunks gated) across layers by union bound, or combine layers multiplicatively as e-values so the product is still an e-value.
4. For CUSUM, choose h by simulating the in-control ARL on the benign stream until ARL_0 is at least the desired number of chunks between false alarms; for the martingale, alarm at 1/alpha.
5. Re-run the recorded red-team payloads (the sandbox's Gumbel-Softmax attacker) to measure detection rate at the chosen operating point, then repeat with the attacker given white-box access to every layer, since Nasr et al. show static evaluations understate ASR by an order of magnitude.

**What an adaptive attacker can still do, per layer.**
- Layer 0: craft fluent, natural-entropy, normally compressible text (AutoDAN, N-gram results, synonym substitution against MEMSAD).
- Layer 1: keep ||g_t|| under the robust threshold and choose directions orthogonal to g_c and g_h, then rely on accumulation (Pang et al. 2021) or on curvature coupling to reach the harmful region without ever aligning with the probe gradients.
- Layer 2: the Fisher is estimated on benign data, so directions the benign stream never exercises look "unimportant" and cost nothing under the penalty; a slowly poisoned calibration stream also corrupts F itself.
- Layer 3: projection is first-order and local; the alignment-collapse paper shows second-order acceleration redirects trajectories despite satisfied half-space constraints; with a small fixed canary the attacker can also learn what the canary covers and aim beside it.
- Layer 4: a budget bounds damage per verification interval but not damage per se; an attacker that stays under budget and waits for the anchor to be re-committed (a poisoned commit) makes the drift permanent. Verification quality at commit time is the real limit.
- Layer 5: rollback needs a probe that responds; sample-wise targeted attacks and trigger-conditioned harm leave the canary and holdout loss unchanged. If the holdout is inferred, the attacker adds explicit terms to keep L_H flat. Also, the SQDP result shows some corrupted states are not recoverable by benign data, so rollback must be to a stored anchor, not "wait for healing".
- Layer 6: a replay adversary (echo chamber) and label-distribution-matched poisons produce streams that are exchangeable with benign traffic by construction; sub-k drift stays below CUSUM's reference value indefinitely, and the theoretical result is that a linear fraction of unbounded poison defeats every regularization-based defense.

Net: the stack turns "is this text suspicious" into "would this update move the model, in Fisher metric, toward a state where the private probes get worse, faster than benign traffic ever does", with anytime-valid alarms and a hard budget as the backstop. Its irreducible dependence is on the secrecy and coverage of the probe sets and on the integrity of the benign calibration stream; both should be treated as the crown jewels and rotated.
